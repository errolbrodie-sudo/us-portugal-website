Relocating to Portugal with Pets

A complete compliance guide for US citizens moving dogs and cats to Portugal, outlining microchips, veterinary certificates, timelines, and costs.

Last Updated: September 3, 2026

Essential Entry Requirements

Moving a pet to the European Union (and Portugal specifically) requires meticulous preparation to satisfy strict sanitary and veterinary import rules. Compliance is mandatory to avoid customs delays or quarantine.

1. ISO Microchip Requirement

Your pet must be implanted with a 15-digit ISO 11784/11785 compliant microchip. The microchip must be non-encrypted. If your pet has a 9-digit or 10-digit microchip, your vet must implant a new ISO-compliant chip, or you must carry your own microchip scanner at the border.

Critical: The microchip must be implanted before or on the same day as the qualifying rabies vaccine.

2. Rabies Vaccination Rules

Pets must have a valid rabies vaccination administered after the microchip implantation. If it is the pet’s primary rabies vaccine (or if their previous vaccine expired), you must wait at least 21 days before they are allowed to travel. Both 1-year and 3-year vaccines are acceptable in Portugal as long as they are currently valid.

3. EU Health Certificate & U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA) Endorsement

You must obtain a non-commercial EU Health Certificate (known as Annex IV). This certificate must be completed by a USDA-accredited veterinarian and then endorsed by the USDA Animal and Plant Health Inspection Service (APHIS). The certificate is valid for travel for 10 days from the date of USDA endorsement until arrival at the EU entry point.

4. Portuguese Border Notification (DGAV)

At least 48 hours before arrival, you must notify the Portuguese veterinary authority (DGAV - Direção-Geral de Alimentação e Veterinária) and the airport's border inspection post of your entry date, flight number, and pet documentation.

Breed Restrictions, Leash Laws & Local Documentation

1. Potentially Dangerous Breeds

Portugal has strict laws governing breeds classified as "potentially dangerous." These breeds include:

  • Rottweiler
  • Fila Brasileiro (Brazilian Mastiff)
  • Pit Bull Terrier
  • Staffordshire Bull Terrier
  • American Staffordshire Terrier
  • Dogo Argentino (Argentinian Mastiff)
  • Tosa Inu

If you own one of these breeds (or crossbreeds thereof), you must register them with the local parish (Junta de Freguesia) within 30 days of arrival. You must also sign a liability statement, secure a specific pet liability insurance policy, undergo physical/psychological evaluations, and have a local Portuguese representative or guarantor if requested. When in public spaces, these dogs must wear a muzzle and be kept on a short leash (under 1 meter).

2. Recommended Additional Vaccines

Beside the mandatory Rabies vaccine, the following vaccines are highly recommended for dogs moving to Portugal:

  • DHPP (Distemper, Hepatitis, Parvovirus, Parainfluenza)
  • Leptospirosis
  • Leishmaniasis protection: Prevalent in Portugal due to sandflies. Use specialized collars (like Scalibor) or spot-on treatments.

3. Landlord & Lease Authorization

While Portuguese law generally allows tenants to keep pets, many landlords include clauses requiring explicit pet authorization, pet security deposits, or third-party liability insurance to cover property damage. Having a local legal representative can help negotiate these clauses.

Chronological Relocation Timeline

We recommend beginning the compliance process at least 4 months before your target departure.

1
4 Months Before Departure: Microchip & Baseline Vaccine

Implant an ISO-compliant 15-digit microchip. Administer or update the Rabies vaccine. Ensure the chip number is noted on all medical charts.

2
3 Months Before Departure: Carrier Training & Airline Booking

Purchase an IATA-approved travel carrier (for dogs/cats). Start training your pet to sleep and rest inside it. Contact airlines to secure a space (cabin weight limits are usually 8kg/17lbs including the carrier; larger pets must travel as manifest cargo or checked baggage).

3
1 Month Before Departure: Vet Coordination

Schedule your USDA-accredited vet appointment within 10 days of your flight. Verify weather restrictions (airlines will not fly cargo pets if temperatures exceed 85°F/29°C at any layover point).

4
10 Days Before Departure: Health Certificate & Endorsement

Attend the vet appointment to issue the EU Health Certificate. Submit the paperwork electronically via the USDA Veterinary Export Health Certification System (VEHCS) portal to obtain the official APHIS seal.

5
48 Hours Before Departure: DGAV Notification

Send the DGAV entry notification form to your arrival airport’s vet inspection post (Lisbon, Porto, or Faro) with PDF copies of your flight ticket and endorsed health certificate.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can puppies or kittens travel? ▼
Portugal does not permit the entry of young puppies or kittens that are unvaccinated against rabies. Pets must be at least 12 weeks old to receive their rabies vaccine, followed by a mandatory 21-day waiting period. Therefore, the youngest age a puppy or kitten can legally enter Portugal from the US is 15 weeks old.
What if the veterinary paperwork is incomplete? ▼
If paperwork is incomplete or the rabies vaccine was administered prior to the microchip implantation, border inspectors may place the pet in quarantine (at your expense), deport the pet back to the US, or, in extreme cases, euthanize the pet. Ensuring USDA endorsement signatures are completed within the 10-day window is critical.
Can multiple pets travel together? ▼
Yes. Under non-commercial rules, an individual can import up to 5 pets. If you are importing more than 5 pets, the shipment is classified as commercial, which requires a distinct import license, commercial certificates, and entry via custom-cleared commercial ports.
Are Emotional Support Animals (ESAs) treated differently? ▼
Yes. The European Union does not recognize Emotional Support Animals (ESAs) for air travel. ESAs are treated as standard household pets and must conform to cargo/cabin size restrictions and regular pet airline fees. Only officially certified service dogs (such as guide dogs for the blind) are granted free cabin access on transatlantic flights.
What happens if my pet arrives in Portugal without the mandatory 48-hour DGAV border pre-notification? ▼
Arriving without notifying the Portuguese veterinary authority (DGAV) 48 hours in advance will trigger border veterinary inspection delays, an expedited emergency inspection fee (approx. €40–€100 per pet), and potential temporary holding at airport customs until an official DGAV vet is dispatched.
